### Is the US Economy in the "Overheat" Phase?

**Verdict: Confirmed — The U.S. economy is solidly in the Overheat phase (48.5°), characterized by above-trend growth coupled with persistent, above-target inflation.**

- **Recovery:** Growth > Trend, Inflation < Trend / Target (Quadrant IV: 270° to 360°/0°)
- **Overheat:** Growth > Trend, Inflation > Trend / Target (Quadrant I: 0° to 90°)
- **Stagflation:** Growth < Trend, Inflation > Trend / Target (Quadrant II: 90° to 180°)
- **Reflation:** Growth < Trend, Inflation < Trend / Target (Quadrant III: 180° to 270°)

With the **Composite Growth Z-score at +0.720** and the **Composite Inflation Z-score at +0.813**, both cyclical dimensions remain firmly positive:

Positioned at 48.5°, the economic clock hand sits directly in the center of Quadrant I (the 1:37 clock position), continuing its orderly clockwise trajectory along the Overheat arc toward late-cycle Stagflation (90°).

Monetary policy remains in restrictive territory, with the Fed carefully calibrating its tightening trajectory:

#### 2. FOMC Minutes (Released October 7, 2026)

- **Consensus for Further Tightening:** A clear majority of participants continue to judge that another 25 bps rate hike will likely be appropriate before the end of 2026, targeting a terminal policy rate of **4.00%–4.25%**, to ensure that above-target inflation expectations do not become entrenched.
- **Prudence at the October Meeting:** However, given recent moderation in monthly labor additions and the high level of long-term bond yields, policymakers indicated a willingness to "skip" or pause at the **October 27–28, 2026** meeting. Markets have priced the probability of an October rate hike down to roughly **18%–20%**, expecting the committee to wait for additional fourth-quarter inflation data before delivering a final move in December.
- **Balance Sheet Runoff (QT):** Quantitative tightening continues at its steady pace, draining bank reserves and maintaining pressure on duration assets.

#### 3. Bond Market Response: 10-Year Yield at 5.24%
The benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yield touched a 52-week peak of 5.31% on October 5 before pulling back to **5.24%** on October 9. The consolidation reflects:
- Market pricing of a likely October FOMC pause.
- Persistent heavy Treasury coupon supply and elevated term premiums.
- The realization that despite potential pauses, rate cuts are entirely absent from the near-term horizon.

#### Alignment with the Overheat Phase
In standard macroeconomic frameworks, an Overheat regime features positive output gaps and sticky inflation, requiring restrictive monetary policy. The Fed's stance—holding rates high, leaving the door open to 4.00%–4.25%, and maintaining QT—perfectly matches textbook Overheat behavior.

1. **Primary Asset Class: Commodities (Overweight)**
   Commodities are the dominant asset class in Overheat. Physical supply constraints, steady real demand (GDPNow 3.6%, ISM 54.5%), and wholesale price pressures (PPI 5.44%) generate high risk-adjusted returns and vital inflation hedging.
2. **Secondary Asset Class: Equities (Neutral / Selective)**
   Equities benefit from nominal revenue expansion, but valuation multiples are constrained by 5.24% Treasury yields. Portfolio exposure should concentrate in cash-generative cyclical sectors with robust pricing power.
3. **Third Asset Class: Cash (Tactical Underweight / Selective Preservation)**
   Yielding 3.85%–4.10% across money market instruments, cash provides downside protection and flexibility without duration risk.
4. **Underweight: Government Bonds (Avoid / Short Duration)**
   With 10-year yields trading around 5.24% and the Fed signaling higher-for-longer rates with another potential hike before year-end, long-duration fixed income remains vulnerable to further capital losses.
