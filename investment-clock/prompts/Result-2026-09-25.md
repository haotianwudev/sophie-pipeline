## 3. FEDERAL RESERVE & MONETARY POLICY

### The FOMC Decision & Policy Stance (September 2026)

Monetary policy shifted into an active tightening mode on September 16, 2026:

#### 1. The 25 bps Rate Hike (Target Range: 3.75%–4.00%)
The FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%**, representing the central bank's first rate increase in over three years.

#### 2. Chair Kevin Warsh's Strategic Doctrine
- **Removal of Policy Accommodation:** Chair Warsh stated unequivocally that strong domestic growth (+5.0% GDPNow) and persistent inflation (3.34% Core PCE, 5.44% PPI) necessitated removing monetary accommodation.
- **Rejection of Calendar Guidance:** Emphasizing genuine data dependence, Warsh abstained from submitting an individual SEP dot, warning that rigid forward guidance risks misleading markets and fueling speculative excess.
- **Defending Policy Independence:** Warsh resisted political commentary from the executive branch advocating for rate cuts, stressing that preserving the Fed's 2.0% inflation credibility is non-negotiable.
- **SEP Dot Plot Projections:** The median FOMC participant projects **at least one additional 25 bps rate hike before year-end 2026**, bringing the expected terminal rate to 4.00%–4.25%.

#### 3. Bond Market Reaction & 10-Year Yield at 5.18%
Following the hike, the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield surged to a cycle peak of **5.18%** on September 24–25. This move reflects an expanding term premium, heavy federal debt issuance, and market realization that the Fed will maintain restrictive rates well into 2027.

#### Alignment with the Overheat Phase
In textbook Investment Clock theory, the Overheat phase requires central banks to raise interest rates to suppress demand-pull inflation and lean against wage/commodity spirals. The Fed's rate hike into 5.0% GDP growth represents a classic, disciplined Overheat monetary response.
