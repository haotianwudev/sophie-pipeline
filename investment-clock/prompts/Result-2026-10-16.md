### Is the US Economy in the "Overheat" Phase?

**Verdict: Confirmed — The U.S. economy is solidly in the Overheat phase (47.0°), characterized by above-trend growth paired with intense, above-target inflation.**

Under the Merrill Lynch Investment Clock framework:
- **Recovery:** Growth > Trend, Inflation < Trend / Target (Quadrant IV: 270° to 360°/0°)
- **Overheat:** Growth > Trend, Inflation > Trend / Target (Quadrant I: 0° to 90°)
- **Stagflation:** Growth < Trend, Inflation > Trend / Target (Quadrant II: 90° to 180°)
- **Reflation:** Growth < Trend, Inflation < Trend / Target (Quadrant III: 180° to 270°)

With the **Composite Growth Z-score at +0.874** and the **Composite Inflation Z-score at +0.939**, both dimensions sit well above their neutral thresholds ($Z > 0$):

Positioned at 47.0°, the economic clock hand sits directly in the active center of Quadrant I (the ~1:34 clock position), advancing along the Overheat arc toward late-cycle Stagflation (90°).

## 3. FEDERAL RESERVE & MONETARY POLICY

### The FOMC Policy Stance & Recent Communications

Monetary policy remains firmly in restrictive territory as the central bank navigates resilient output and sticky price pressures:

#### 1. The Policy Rate Baseline (3.75%–4.00%)
Following the 25 bps rate hike on September 16, 2026, the target range for the federal funds rate stands at **3.75%–4.00%**. This increase reasserted the FOMC's commitment to containing price pressures after mid-year data showed Core PCE plateauing above 3.0%.

#### 2. Chair Warsh's Remarks and Fed Communications (Mid-October 2026)
- **IMF Annual Meetings Address:** Speaking at the IMF annual meetings in Bangkok in mid-October 2026, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh acknowledged that inflation remains "uncomfortably elevated" and that the Committee cannot tolerate above-target inflation becoming embedded in business planning. However, Warsh downplayed immediate market speculation of an automatic back-to-back rate hike at the upcoming **October 27–28 FOMC meeting**, emphasizing that the FOMC will proceed "meeting by meeting" based on incoming data.
- **Hawkish Undercurrents:** Concurrently, Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson delivered remarks noting that persistent upside inflation risks and resilient economic growth may require additional policy firming before the end of the year.
- **Market Pricing:** Fed funds futures price an ~18% probability of a rate hike at the October 27–28 meeting, but price a ~65% probability of a final 25 bps hike to **4.00%–4.25%** at the December 15–16 meeting.
- **Balance Sheet Runoff:** Quantitative tightening (QT) proceeds without interruption, continuing to drain liquidity from the financial system and maintaining upward pressure on term premiums.

#### 3. Bond Market Response: 10-Year Yield at 5.08%–5.10%
The benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury note yield closed the week of October 16 at **5.08%–5.10%**, consolidating approximately 14–16 basis points from the 5.24% recorded on October 9 and the cycle high of 5.31% reached on October 5. This modest rally was propelled by:
- Chair Warsh's signals of a probable pause at the October FOMC meeting.
- Technical consolidation following sharp multi-week sell-offs in duration assets.
- However, with yields firmly above 5.00%, corporate borrowing costs, commercial real estate loans, and mortgage rates (~7.4%–7.6%) continue to exert significant restraint on interest-sensitive sectors.

#### Alignment with the Overheat Phase
In an Overheat regime, real output outstrips potential while price pressures build. The Fed's policy stance—holding rates restrictive, maintaining a tightening bias toward 4.00%–4.25%, and continuing balance sheet reduction—is entirely consistent with the Merrill Lynch framework.
