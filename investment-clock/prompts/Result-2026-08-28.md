## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The U.S. economy as of late August 2026 firmly occupies the **Recovery** phase of the Merrill Lynch Investment Clock, defined by above-trend growth (Composite Growth Z-score: +0.257) combined with below-trend inflation (Composite Inflation Z-score: -0.340). This "Goldilocks" configuration is supported by robust real GDP growth (GDPNow Q3 2026: 4.6% annualized), strong ISM Manufacturing PMI (55.6 in July — highest since May 2022), a healthy labor market (unemployment 4.1%, jobless claims ~203k), and fading inflation relative to prior Overheat readings. However, meaningful tensions exist: Core PCE at 3.3% YoY and headline CPI at 3.4% YoY remain materially above the Fed's 2% target. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, speaking at Jackson Hole today (August 28, 2026), delivered a hawkish tone, driving market-implied probability of a September rate *hike* to ~60%. The 6-month trajectory (clockwise rotation from Overheat Q2 → Recovery now) is clear, but the inflation Z-score is climbing back toward zero, signaling clockwise momentum that could push the clock toward Overheat within 1–2 quarters if price pressures re-accelerate. **Equities** remain the preferred asset class, with Technology, Telecom, and Materials as the strongest sector tilts for this phase.

## 1. PHASE VALIDATION: Is the Economy in "Recovery"?

### Supporting Evidence

**Growth signals are firmly above-trend:**
- **Atlanta Fed GDPNow** (as of August 26, 2026): Q3 2026 real GDP growth estimated at **+4.6% annualized** — a robust expansion rate well above the ~2% long-run trend.
- **Industrial Production (INDPRO):** 102.99, a positive coincident signal indicating ongoing output expansion.
- **OECD CLI:** 99.85 (just below the expansion threshold of 100), which signals the economy is operating near full capacity without excessive acceleration.
- **ISM Manufacturing PMI (July 2026):** 55.6 — the strongest factory expansion reading since May 2022. The 25th consecutive month of expansion in ISM Services (54.1 in July) confirms broad-based activity.
- **Initial Jobless Claims (week ending Aug 22):** 203,000 — near historic lows, confirming labor market resilience with no signs of broad layoffs. 4-week moving average: 205,500.
- **S&P 500 YTD:** +12.8% through late August 2026, with +3.0% in August alone — equity markets corroborating a supportive macro backdrop.

**Inflation signals are below-trend (disinflationary):**
- **Composite Inflation Z-score: -0.340** — below-trend, confirming the disinflation narrative relative to recent Overheat readings.
- **5-Year Breakeven Inflation:** 2.26% — market-implied forward inflation only modestly above the 2% target; a far cry from the 3%+ levels seen during the Overheat phase of 2026 Q2.
- **Core CPI YoY:** 2.15% — close to the Fed's target, suggesting underlying consumer price pressures are well-anchored at the consumer level.
- **CPI MoM Annualized:** 2.62% — slightly above target but not accelerating materially.
- **Capacity Utilization (TCU):** 76.3% — moderate, indicating demand-pull pressures are present but not overheating the supply side.

**Contradictions / Tensions:**
- **PPI Final Demand YoY:** 4.99% — pipeline producer price inflation remains very elevated relative to consumer-level CPI, suggesting upstream price pressures could transmit into consumer prices with a lag. This is the single largest quantitative red flag.
- **Core PCE (July 2026):** 3.3% YoY (+0.2% MoM) — the Fed's *preferred* measure remains 130 bps above target with no clear downward momentum ("grinding sideways").
- **Headline CPI (July 2026):** 3.4% YoY — well above 2% target.

**Overall Assessment:** The quantitative framework correctly identifies the current configuration as **Recovery**: above-trend growth, below-trend inflation relative to the Overheat peak seen in Q1-Q2 2026. The inflation decline is real but incomplete — and the twin risks are (a) re-acceleration from PPI pipeline and (b) hawkish Fed policy error that chills growth.

## 3. FEDERAL RESERVE & MONETARY POLICY

### Current Stance: Fed Funds Target Rate 3.50%–3.75% (held at July 28-29, 2026 meeting)

**Jackson Hole — August 28, 2026 — Chair Kevin Warsh:**
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh (who replaced Jerome Powell in May 2026) delivered his first major keynote at the Jackson Hole Symposium today, August 28, 2026.
- **Key Message:** Inflation has not improved sufficiently. Quote: *"We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do."*
- Warsh expressed confidence in the economy's strength but made clear the Fed's "predominant focus right now should be on prices."
- **Communication Style:** Warsh explicitly moved away from traditional forward guidance, preferring data-dependent responses over signaling. He argued the Fed "should not indulge a regime in which market participants are looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade."
- **Market Reaction:** Post-speech, the market-implied probability of a **rate hike** at the September 15–16 meeting rose to **~60%** (from ~35% the prior day). This represents a hawkish pivot in market pricing.

**Alignment with Recovery Phase:**
The Recovery phase typically calls for accommodative (easing) monetary policy to support growth while inflation is contained. The current configuration is *partially misaligned*:
- The Fed maintained rates at 3.50%–3.75%, which is restrictive relative to the neutral rate (~2.5–3.0%).
- A potential September rate hike would further tighten into a Recovery phase — unusual and a significant headwind risk.
- The hawkish Warsh tone suggests the Fed is more concerned about re-acceleration of inflation (PPI, sticky PCE) than about supporting growth. This is a tension that increases the risk of a policy error.

**Key Divergence:** In a standard Recovery, the Fed would be easing or on hold. Here, the Fed is on hold-to-tightening. This increases the risk of a mid-cycle correction if rate hikes compress the yield curve or dampen investment.

### Multi-Metric Scorecard (July 2026, latest available)

|-----------|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| Core CPI YoY | 2.15% | ↓ Moderating | +15bps above target |
| Core PCE YoY | 3.3% | → Sideways | +130bps above target |
| Headline CPI YoY | 3.4% | ↓ From ~4% peak | +140bps above target |
| PPI Final Demand YoY | 4.99% (July: 4.7%) | ↓ Declining | Highly elevated |
| 5Y Breakeven Inflation | 2.26% | → Stable | +26bps above target |
| CPI MoM Annualized | 2.62% | → Stable | +62bps above target |

**Narrative Analysis:**
1. **Consumer-level inflation (CPI) is the bright spot** — Core CPI at 2.15% is tantalizingly close to 2%, confirming that the consumer price pressures from the 2025-early 2026 Overheat phase have substantially moderated.
2. **Core PCE remains stubbornly sticky** — at 3.3%, it has been "grinding sideways" for several months (per KPMG research). Tariffs are estimated to have added 0.2–0.4 percentage points to core PCE, meaning ex-tariff inflation is closer to 2.9–3.1%.
3. **PPI is the major warning signal** — even after declining to 4.7% YoY in July (from 5.5% in June), wholesale prices remain at levels not typically consistent with a sustained Recovery phase. If economic activity accelerates, producers may begin passing these costs through to consumers, triggering CPI re-acceleration.
4. **Inflation breakevens** at 2.26% are contained, suggesting bond markets don't expect runaway inflation — this is a stabilizing signal.

**Conclusion on Inflation:** The inflation trajectory has improved dramatically from the Overheat peak but remains above-target at multiple levels. The disinflationary impulse is real but fragile. The clock hand is correctly positioned at -0.340 Inflation Z — below trend but rising back toward zero.

## 7. KEY RISKS & DISRUPTIONS

### Exogenous Shock Risks

**1. Fed Policy Error (HIGH PROBABILITY)**
The most significant near-term risk. Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech has pushed September rate-hike probability to ~60%. If the Fed raises rates into a Recovery phase with inflation showing clear moderation (Core CPI: 2.15%), it risks:
- Inverting the yield curve (currently +47bps spread)
- Suppressing business investment (already seeing "wait-and-see" behavior)
- Slowing consumer spending
- A premature mid-cycle correction that could reverse the clockwise trajectory

**2. PPI Inflation Re-Acceleration (MEDIUM-HIGH PROBABILITY)**
PPI Final Demand remains at 4.99% (composite indicator value) despite July easing to 4.7% YoY. If upstream costs begin transmitting to consumer prices, Core PCE could re-accelerate, pushing the inflation Z-score back above zero and triggering a faster clockwise rotation into Overheat. Combined with a hawkish Fed, this could create a Stagflation scare.

**3. Trade Policy Uncertainty (MEDIUM PROBABILITY)**
While the Supreme Court struck down certain IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 (reducing effective tariff rate from ~10% to ~6.7%), trade policy remains fluid. Additional Section 301 tariffs or escalating bilateral trade disputes could:
- Add 0.2–0.4 percentage points to Core PCE (already happening)
- Disrupt supply chains and depress manufacturing sentiment
- Create a counter-clockwise shock toward Stagflation if both growth and inflation are negatively impacted

**4. Geopolitical Energy Shock (LOW-MEDIUM PROBABILITY)**
Ongoing Middle East tensions and resource competition (critical minerals for AI/EV) create tail risks to energy prices. A sharp oil price spike (e.g., via Strait of Hormuz disruption) could:
- Push headline CPI materially higher
- Compress consumer purchasing power
- Force the clock counter-clockwise from Recovery toward Stagflation

**5. AI Capex Disappointment (LOW PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT)**
The U.S. growth and equity bull case is heavily concentrated in AI infrastructure spending. If leading AI companies (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, NVIDIA) announce capex cuts or revenue misses, the market could experience a sharp de-rating of tech multiples, removing the primary driver of economic momentum.

**6. Labor Market Deterioration (LOW PROBABILITY)**
July NFP at -23,000 is a data point to watch. The "no hire, no fire" dynamic could shift if corporate margin pressures intensify. A sustained rise in jobless claims above 250,000 would signal a growth inflection downward.

## 8. HISTORICAL ANALOGS

### Closest Historical Parallels to Current Z-Score Configuration (+0.26 Growth, -0.34 Inflation)

**Best Analog: 1995–1996 ("Goldilocks" Mid-Cycle)**
- **Context:** After the Fed's aggressive 1994 rate-hike cycle, inflation moderated while growth remained robust. GDP grew ~3% annualized, CPI fell below 3%, and the Fed *cut* rates once in 1995.
- **Clock Position:** Recovery. Similar Growth/Inflation Z-score configuration.
- **Market Outcome:** S&P 500 gained +34% in 1995, driven by Technology and large-cap growth. The clock gradually moved clockwise into Overheat as the dot-com boom took hold by 1997–1998.
- **Key Difference from 2026:** The 1995 Fed *eased* into Recovery; Warsh's Fed is on hold-to-tightening. This is a meaningful divergence that makes 2026 more challenging than 1995.

**Secondary Analog: 2016–2017 (Post-Earnings Recession Recovery)**
- **Context:** After the 2015 manufacturing recession and global growth scare (driven by China concerns), the U.S. economy re-accelerated in 2016. ISM Manufacturing crossed back above 50 in late 2016. Inflation was rising from post-crisis lows.
- **Clock Position:** Recovery/early Overheat transition in late 2016 through 2017.
- **Market Outcome:** S&P 500 +12% in 2016, +19% in 2017. Cyclicals (Industrials, Materials, Financials) dramatically outperformed. The clock moved clockwise into Overheat by early 2018.
- **Relevance to 2026:** Strong manufacturing PMI (55.6 in July 2026 mirrors the 2016-17 manufacturing revival), equity markets broadening, and clockwise rotation all align. However, 2026 has a higher starting rate environment and more geopolitical complexity.

**Tertiary Analog: 2010–2011 (Post-GFC Recovery)**
- **Context:** QE-supported recovery with above-trend growth and below-trend inflation (deflation fear in 2009-10 transitioned to rising inflation by 2011). Fed was deeply accommodative.
- **Market Outcome:** S&P 500 +15% in 2010, driven by Technology, Materials, and Energy. By 2011, inflation concerns began creating Overheat dynamics.
- **Key Difference:** The 2010 Fed was at the zero lower bound and actively easing. In contrast, 2026 rates at 3.5–3.75% are restrictive, making the growth outlook more tenuous.

**Common Thread Across Analogs:**
In all three periods, the transition from Recovery to Overheat occurred within 6–12 months once above-trend growth was confirmed and inflation bottomed. Equities — particularly Technology and Cyclicals — delivered the strongest returns during the Recovery phase itself. The critical variable in 2026 vs. all analogs is the *starting point of monetary policy* — a tightening Fed in Recovery is historically unusual and bears watching.

## CONCLUSION

The U.S. economy is firmly in the **Recovery** phase of the Merrill Lynch Investment Clock as of August 28, 2026. The quantitative composite signals (Growth Z: +0.257, Inflation Z: -0.340) align with the algorithmic determination. The evidence base — GDPNow at 4.6%, ISM Manufacturing at 55.6 (strongest since 2022), low jobless claims (203k), non-inverted yield curve, S&P 500 +12.8% YTD — unambiguously confirms above-trend growth. Inflation, while still above the Fed's 2% target in absolute terms (Core PCE: 3.3%), has decelerated dramatically from Overheat levels, placing it below trend relative to the 6-month trajectory.

The clock is moving **clockwise** at a moderate pace. The primary risk is that (a) the Fed tightens prematurely via a September rate hike, or (b) PPI pipeline inflation reignites consumer prices, forcing an earlier-than-expected Overheat transition. The balance of evidence favors continued Recovery with a 45% probability of transitioning to Overheat within the next 1–2 quarters. **Equities** (Technology, Telecom, Materials) remain the preferred asset class, supported by both quantitative signals and strong earnings growth. The historical analog to 1995–96 and 2016–17 supports continuation of the bull market in equities through at least Q4 2026, with a key inflection point dependent on the September FOMC decision and subsequent inflation data.
